5 Common Document Scanning Mistakes to Avoid

Have you ever received a scanned document from someone and thought, "How did they manage to make it look so bad?" Or maybe you've sent a scan yourself and later realized it was completely unreadable.

It happens to the best of us. Scanning seems like a simple task, but a few small mistakes can turn a perfectly good document into a useless, blurry mess. Over the years, I've made every mistake in the book (and learned from them). Here are the top 5 mistakes I see people make all the time.

Mistake #1: Ignoring the Lighting

This is the biggest culprit. Most people just take a photo wherever they are sitting. If there is a window behind you, your shadow falls right across the document. If there is a bright light above, it creates a glare on the paper.

The Fix: Always use diffused, natural daylight. Stand near a window but don't block the light. Never use the camera flash.

Mistake #2: Not Flattening the Paper

If you are scanning a crumpled receipt or a folded letter, the text on the folds gets distorted. OCR tools struggle a lot with curved or folded text.

The Fix: Take 10 seconds to smooth out the paper with your hand. For very stubborn folds, place a heavy book on top of the document for a few minutes before scanning.

Mistake #3: Saving as the Wrong Format

A lot of people just save their scans as JPEG images. While that works for photos, it's not ideal for documents. JPEG compresses the image, which can make the text look fuzzy.

The Fix: Always save your scanned documents as a PDF. PDFs keep the text sharp and maintain the layout perfectly, no matter what device opens them. Tools like DocuSwift Scan let you download directly as a PDF.

Mistake #4: Forgetting to Use OCR

Many people just save the image and think that's enough. But an image is not searchable. If you scan a 50-page report as an image, you can't search for a specific word. Always run OCR on your document. It takes 5 extra seconds and makes your file completely searchable. Tools like DocuSwift Scan have built-in OCR that makes this super easy.

Mistake #5: Bad File Naming

If you save a file as "Scan_001.pdf" or "New Document.pdf", you will forget what it is within a week. Later, when you need it urgently, you will be stuck opening dozens of files to find the right one.

The Fix: Always name your files properly. Use the format: Date_Type_Details. For example: 2026-01-20_Receipt_Amazon.pdf or 2026-02-15_Contract_Signed.pdf. This one habit will save you hours of frustration.

Conclusion

Scanning documents doesn't have to be complicated. Avoid these 5 common mistakes, and you will get clean, professional, and useful digital files every single time. Remember: good lighting, flat paper, PDF format, OCR text, and proper naming.

Give it a try with your next document and see the difference!
