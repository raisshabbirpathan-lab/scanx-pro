How to Improve OCR Accuracy: 7 Tips for Perfect Text Extraction

You found a great OCR tool. You uploaded your document. You clicked "Extract Text" with high hopes. And then... the text comes out looking like a five-year-old typed it. Words are jumbled, letters are missing, and it is a complete mess.

What went wrong? OCR (Optical Character Recognition) is not magic. It is a computer algorithm that tries to recognize patterns. Think of it like a human eye—if you give it a blurry, dark, or crooked image, it will struggle to read it.

The good news is that you don't need a professional scanner to get perfect results. You just need to follow a few simple rules. Here are 7 tips that will instantly improve your OCR accuracy.

1. Use a High-Resolution Image

This is the number one reason OCR fails. If you take a low-quality photo with a cheap phone, the tool has very little data to work with. Always try to use an image that is at least 300 DPI (dots per inch). If you are taking a photo, make sure your camera settings are set to the highest quality. Do not send screenshots of documents if you can avoid it.

2. Perfect Your Lighting

Shadows are the enemy of OCR. If half of your document is in a shadow and the other half is bright, the algorithm gets confused. It tries to figure out if that dark patch is a letter or just a smudge. Always scan in a well-lit room, preferably using natural daylight, and avoid casting your own shadow over the paper.

3. Keep the Document Flat

If you are scanning a book or a crumpled receipt, the text is probably curved. OCR works best when the text is straight and flat. If you are scanning a book, try to press it down gently or use a glass sheet to flatten it. For receipts, you might need to iron them out on a low setting first (just be careful!).

4. Use the Enhancement Features

This is why we built image enhancement tools into DocuSwift Scan. Before you run OCR, take a moment to adjust the image. Increasing the contrast slightly makes the text darker and the background whiter. Applying a Black & White filter can strip away background noise, making it much easier for the OCR engine to find the letters.

5. Choose the Right Language

It sounds simple, but a lot of people forget this step. If your document is in Hindi, but you set the OCR tool to English, it will try to force Hindi characters into English ones. The result is gibberish. Always double-check your language settings before hitting the extract button.

Bonus Tips for Difficult Documents

  • Handwriting: Standard OCR tools struggle with handwritten text. You might need a specialized tool for that.
  • Fancy Fonts: Decorative or script fonts are hard to read. Stick to standard fonts like Arial or Times New Roman for the best results.
  • Proofread: Even with perfect settings, OCR is never 100% accurate. Always do a quick proofread before sending that important document.

Conclusion

Getting perfect OCR results is less about finding a magic tool and more about giving the tool the best possible input. By following these simple tips, you can take your text extraction accuracy from a frustrating 70% to a highly reliable 95% or more.

Next time you scan a document, take an extra 10 seconds to fix the lighting and adjust the contrast. Your OCR results will thank you.
