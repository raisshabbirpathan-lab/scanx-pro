How to Convert Image to Text on Mobile: A Complete Guide

Have you ever taken a photo of a document, a receipt, or a page from a book, and then realized you actually needed the text from it to edit or send?

It happens to me all the time. You think, "Oh, I'll just take a picture," but then you end up typing the whole thing out manually because you can't copy text from a picture. It's frustrating and a total waste of time.

The good news is, you don't have to do that anymore. Your phone can actually read the text from images. This technology is called OCR (Optical Character Recognition).

Today, I am going to show you exactly how to convert an image to text on your mobile phone, step by step.

Step 1: Take a Proper Photo

Before you even think about extracting text, you need a good image. OCR works best when the text is clear.

Here is what you should keep in mind:

  • Lighting: Turn on the lights or stand near a window. Avoid using flash, as it creates a bright spot in the middle of the page.
  • Angle: Stand directly above the document. If you take a photo from an angle, the text will look slanted and the tool might get confused.
  • Focus: Tap your screen to focus on the text before clicking the picture. A blurry photo will result in a blurry text output.

Step 3: Extract the Text

Once you upload your image, select the language of the text (like English or Hindi) and click the "Extract Text" button.

The tool will scan the image and pull out all the text. It usually takes just a few seconds. If your image was clear, the text will come out almost perfectly.

Step 4: Copy or Download

Once the text appears on your screen, you can read it, copy it, or even download it as a text file. You can also download the scanned image as a PDF if you need to send it to someone.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a low-quality photo: If the text is too small or too blurry, the tool will give you gibberish. Always use a high-resolution image.
  • Scanning handwritten notes: Standard OCR tools are designed for printed text. They struggle a lot with handwriting. You might need a specialized tool for that.
  • Forgetting to proofread: OCR is never 100% perfect. Always read through the extracted text once before sending it for official work.
