How to Send Scanned Documents Securely via Email

Sending a copy of your passport, a signed contract, or a bank statement over email always gives me a bit of anxiety. It feels like sending a postcard with your bank details written on it—anyone who looks at it can read everything.

A friend of mine once emailed his ID card to the wrong address. It took him months to sort out the mess and prove his identity to the authorities. Don't be like my friend.

If you need to share sensitive documents via email, there are a few simple steps you can take to protect yourself. Here is a simple guide to sending scanned documents securely.

Step 1: Scan and Prepare the Document

Before you think about sending the file, make sure you have a clean, high-quality scan. Use a free tool like DocuSwift Scan to capture the document and extract any text you need. Our tool runs entirely in your browser, which means your sensitive documents are never uploaded to an external server during the scanning process.

Rename the file properly (like Contract_Signed_2026.pdf) so the recipient knows exactly what it is.

Step 2: Compress Large Files

Email services like Gmail have a limit on attachment size (usually 25MB). If your scanned PDF is too large, you have two options:

  • Compress the PDF: Use an online PDF compressor to reduce the file size without losing too much quality.
  • Use Cloud Storage: Upload the file to Google Drive or Dropbox and share a link instead of attaching the file directly. This is often safer and avoids size limits.

Step 3: Add a Password to the PDF

This is the most important step. If you are sending an important document, you should always password-protect it.

Most PDF readers (like Adobe Acrobat or even free online tools) allow you to set a password on a PDF. When you set a password, the file becomes encrypted. Even if someone intercepts the email, they cannot open the document without the password.

The Golden Rule of Passwords

Never send the password in the same email as the document! Send the document first, and then send the password through a different channel—like a text message, a phone call, or a separate email. This way, even if one email is hacked, the hacker still can't open the file.

Step 4: Double-Check the Recipient

I can't tell you how many times I have typed an email and almost sent it to the wrong person because auto-correct filled in the wrong address. Before you hit "Send," double-check the email address. Read it out loud if you have to. Once it's sent, you can't take it back.

Step 5: Consider Using an Encrypted Email Service

If you are sending extremely sensitive documents regularly (like for legal or medical purposes), consider using an encrypted email service like ProtonMail. These services encrypt the entire email, making it nearly impossible for anyone else to read.

Conclusion

Sending documents via email doesn't have to be risky. By taking a few extra seconds to password-protect your files and verify the recipient, you can protect your personal information from falling into the wrong hands.

Stay safe, and always think twice before hitting that send button!
