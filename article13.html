How to Scan ID Cards and Passports Safely

I think we can all agree that sending a copy of our passport or Aadhaar card over the internet is a scary feeling. It feels like handing over a key to your house to a stranger.

A few months ago, a friend of mine was applying for a rental apartment. He emailed his ID card to a broker without thinking twice. A week later, he started getting spam calls and someone tried to open a bank account in his name. It took him months to sort out the mess.

Identity theft is real, and it usually starts with an unprotected scanned copy of your ID. If you need to scan your passport, driver's license, or any other sensitive document, here is how you can do it safely.

Step 1: Use a Secure Scanner (Not an App)

This is the most important step. Most free scanner apps on the Play Store or App Store upload your image to their server to process it. You have no idea what they do with your data.

Instead, use a browser-based tool like DocuSwift Scan. Because our tool runs entirely inside your web browser, your ID card never leaves your device. The processing happens locally on your phone or computer, so there is no server involved. Your privacy is 100% protected.

Step 2: Take a Clear Photo (But Hide Background)

Place your ID card on a clean, plain surface. Make sure the lighting is good so that all the text and numbers are clearly visible.

  • Avoid Shadows: Shadows can hide important details like your date of birth or ID number.
  • Hide Background Clutter: Make sure no other personal documents (like bills or letters) are visible in the background of the photo.

Step 3: The "Watermark" Trick (Most Important)

Never send a raw scan of your ID. Always add a watermark on top of it. You can do this easily using any free image editor or PDF editor.

Write something like: "This copy is for rental application, dated 15 Jan 2026. Not valid for any other purpose."

Place this watermark right across the middle of the card. If someone tries to misuse this copy later, the watermark will prove that it was only meant for one specific purpose.

Pro Tip: Password Protect the PDF

Before emailing your scanned ID, save it as a PDF and add a password. Most PDF editors allow this for free. Send the password via a separate text message, not in the same email. If a hacker intercepts the email, they still can't open the file.

Step 4: Use a Secure File Sharing Method

Don't just attach your ID to a regular email if you can avoid it. Consider using a service like Google Drive with restricted access, or a self-destructing link service like WeTransfer or Proton Drive. These tools give you more control over who can view the file and for how long.

Step 5: Delete the Files When You're Done

Once the purpose of sending the ID is over (like your rental application is approved), delete the scanned file from your email 'Sent' folder and from your phone's gallery. This reduces the risk of it being accidentally leaked later.

Conclusion

Your identity is your most valuable asset. In today's digital world, it takes just a few seconds for someone to misuse your personal information. By taking a few extra precautions while scanning and sharing your ID, you can protect yourself from a lot of trouble.

Be smart, be safe, and always protect your data.
